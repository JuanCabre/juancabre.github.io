Privacy

This site sets no cookies and loads no fonts or images from other servers. The one exception is a small script that counts visits, described below. Everything else is served from one place.

That place is GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc. As with any web server, GitHub processes your IP address and basic request data when you open a page, for the purpose of delivering the site and keeping the service secure. I have no access to these logs. Details are in the GitHub privacy statement.

To learn how many people visit and which pages they read, I use GoatCounter, a privacy-friendly analytics service operated by Martin Tournoij in Ireland, with servers at Hetzner Online GmbH in Germany and Finland. When you open a page, a script from gc.zgo.at sends GoatCounter the page address and title, the page you came from, and the width of your screen. From the request itself, GoatCounter derives your browser, operating system, language, and country. It does not store your IP address or your full browser identification, sets no cookies, and stores nothing on your device. To tell unique visits apart, it keeps your IP address and browser identification in memory for up to eight hours, linked to a random number, and then discards them. I see only aggregate counts, never individual visitors. Details are in the GoatCounter privacy policy.

The legal basis for these counts is my legitimate interest in knowing how the site is used (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). You can object at any time; the simplest way is a content blocker, which usually stops the script, and the site works the same without it.

Links to publishers, DOI resolvers, and other sites lead to pages outside my control. Their own privacy terms apply once you follow a link.

If you write to me by e-mail, I use your message and address only to answer you.
