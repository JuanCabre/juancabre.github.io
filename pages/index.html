I design communication networks around one question: what does the receiver need in order to perform its task?

For most of its history, communication engineering has worked on one problem, which Shannon described as that of "reproducing at one point either exactly or approximately a message selected at another point." His framework made that problem tractable, and it still carries the Internet today. However, many of the applications that networks now serve do not need the message itself. A robot needs a control decision. A digital twin needs to know whether one particular event occurred. A group of agents needs to know whether they agree.

It is within this context that I do my research. I work on post-Shannon communication, where the network delivers what the task requires instead of every bit, and on network coding, where one code can protect information both in transit and in storage. I care about the path from theory to systems, so much of this work ends in an emulator, a testbed, or a demonstrator.

I understand communication systems as systems that transport, store, and compute information. When we talk about communication systems, we therefore talk about much more than the transport of bits. This is why I am also interested in coded distributed computing, reliable distributed computing, low latency, and in-network computing. Computing itself is changing as well. Beyond the digital substrate, I want to understand what becomes possible with new computing substrates such as analog, biological, and quantum computing.

I grew up in Venezuela, where I studied electrical engineering at Universidad Simón Bolívar. I moved to Denmark for an M.Sc. in wireless communication systems at Aalborg University, and in 2015 I joined TU Dresden, where I received my doctorate in 2022. Today I lead a junior research group at CeTI and coordinate its research area on computing and communication.

Architectures and platforms

Networks as programmable infrastructure

I approach networks as programmable infrastructure in which communication and computing are designed together. Software-defined networking, network function virtualization, and network coding can be combined in the mobile edge cloud to meet the latency and resilience requirements of the Tactile Internet. To test such ideas on commodity hardware, I helped build and evaluate ComNetsEmu, an open-source emulator for software-defined and virtualized networks, and I have taught its use in several IEEE tutorials together with Frank H. P. Fitzek and Fabrizio Granelli.

Network coding

Resilient transport and storage

Can one code protect information both in transit and at rest?

Storage is communication across time, so the codes that make transport reliable can also keep stored information available. OneCode uses a single random linear network code to generate packets, recode them over several hops, and repair the redundancy that is lost when nodes fail. In related work, we study peers at the network edge that cache network-coded content and leave or fail over time, and we define a measure, the grade, of how far the coded cache has degraded.

Post-Shannon communication

Task-oriented communication with identification codes

Does a receiver need the whole message to perform its task?

In message identification, the receiver only checks whether one event of interest occurred, which requires far fewer bits than transmitting the message. We proposed performance metrics for identification codes that are closer to practical systems than the asymptotic bounds, and we wrote a tutorial that opens these codes to communication engineers outside information theory. Together with the Chair of Communication Networks at TU Munich, we applied them to a truck platoon whose vehicles send short tags to verify their local control decisions against an edge server at the base station. In simulation, this hybrid scheme needed about four times less traffic than fully centralized control.

Compression for a purpose

Functional and semantic compression

Functional compression sends what the receiver needs to compute a function of the data, instead of the data itself. We merged the views of several cameras at edge application servers through multilevel semantic compression and mapped the design onto the 3GPP architecture for edge computing in 5G, in collaboration with Nokia. The evaluation uses an open-source prototype on commodity hardware with a simulated network. We also demonstrated functional compression in the control loop of a real balancing robot.

Computing over networks

Function computation over networks

Can nodes test whether they agree without exchanging their full states?

Together with Holger Boche's group at TU Munich, we studied this question for relay networks, and we linked message identification to the computation of a function chosen by the receiver. The same line of work led to a German patent on communication devices and methods for message identification.
